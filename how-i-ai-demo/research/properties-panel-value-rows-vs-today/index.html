Properties panel: value rows read well, and now they need to look clickable

Five practice interviews compared the picked Excalidraw properties panel (one row per property, with its current value) with today's panel. Here are the findings, the quotes behind them, and round 2 of the design.

Fictional, written for the workshop. These are practice study results, not real research: no real participants, no real interviews, and nothing here is real user feedback.

Draft PR: round 2 of the panelThe deckBefore and after

Recommendation

Keep the value rows. Every participant read them as the shape's current settings, and three of five would rather use them. Fix three things first:

  1. Make the rows look like controls. P3 read them as "a receipt", and P2 and P4 were guessing that a row opens.
  2. Let rows stay open, several at once. The two heaviest Excalidraw users felt the extra click on every change.
  3. Rename what people misread. Look becomes Quick style, with a line saying what it sets. Solid, Hatched and Cross-hatched become Filled, Lines and Crossed lines.

Round 2 of the panel does all three. Next, rerun only the questions these changes touch, with both panel orders and a mix of Excalidraw users and people new to it.

Findings and quotes

Ranked by strength. Counts are out of five. Quotes are verbatim and cite the participant, the question and the time.

1 The value rows make the shape readable at a glance Keep

All five read the new panel as a summary of how the shape is drawn right now, and three said words beat today's icons.

This one tells me what it is right now, which I like. Background: transparent. Oh, that's why the box looks empty. That actually answers a question I had about the first one.

P4, Q3, 04:10

Oh, this reads like a spec sheet. Stroke, black. Background, transparent. I can see what the shape is without opening anything, which I really like.

P1, Q3, 04:02

This is easier to read. It's words, not icons.

P3, Q3, 03:50

It's a lot of tiny icons. I'd click the colored squares, I guess. The rows with the squiggly lines, I've never known what those do.

P3, Q2, 02:02

Against it

Honestly, this one I know. Everything's right there. Click, click, done.

P2, Q2, 02:40

I don't love it, but my hands know it.

P5, Q2, 01:58

Round 2: Kept: the rows, their order and their values.

2 The rows don't look like they open Fixed in round 2

Only P1 and P5 were sure a row opens, and P1 noticed the chevrons only because they were looking for them.

It looks like a summary, like a receipt. Nothing says "change me." Now that you say it, I see the little arrows.

P3, Q3, 04:33

It shows the options? I'm guessing.

P4, Q3, 04:55

Make the rows feel more clickable. The arrows are small, and I only noticed them because I was looking for them.

P1, Q7, 13:45

Against it

The little arrows on the right. It looks like an accordion.

P1, Q3, 04:43

Round 2: Each value sits in a field with a clear chevron, so a row reads as a dropdown even in a screenshot.

3 Every change costs one more click, and people who style a lot feel it Fixed in round 2

The two heaviest Excalidraw users gave the lowest ease scores (3 and 4) and asked for the same thing: keep rows open.

When I'm styling thirty boxes, I change color, then width, then maybe the fill. In the first one that's three clicks. Here it's open, pick, open, pick, open, pick. That adds up fast.

P2, Q3, 04:58

Let me keep a row open. Or let me open two at once. If I could leave color open while I click through shapes, most of my complaint goes away.

P2, Q7, 14:03

Split. For daily work, the first one, because I'm fast in it. If the second one let me pin the two or three rows I use, I'd switch.

P5, Q4, 06:05

Against it

The second one looks nicer and I'd probably hand it to someone new. But for me the old one is faster.

P2, Q4, 07:10

Round 2: Several rows can be open at once. They stay open as the selection changes, and the panel remembers them.

4 "Look" doesn't say what the row does Fixed in round 2

Two of five read Look as a preview. Two suggested names people know from other tools.

Look is a strange word for it. Look at what? I thought it was going to preview something.

P2, Q5, 09:05

I didn't get what Look meant at first. I thought it was about how it looks to other people, like a preview. Once I saw the six pictures it made sense: it's a starting style.

P4, Q5, 08:02

Call the Look row something like "Quick styles" so I know it's a shortcut.

P4, Q7, 13:12

Look is fine. Maybe "Style" would be more common, that's what Figma would call it.

P1, Q5, 09:23

Round 2: The row is called Quick style, and opening it starts with one line: "Sets fill, stroke width, stroke style and sloppiness in one click."

5 Three look names trip people up: Solid, Hatched and Cross-hatched Fixed in round 2

The pictures rescue every name, but "Solid" collides with Stroke style's Solid, and "hatched" isn't a word everyone knows.

P3, Q5, 08:42

P3, Q5, 07:48

P4, Q5, 08:02

P3, Q7, 12:30

Against it

P5, Q5, 07:50

Round 2: Solid becomes Filled, Hatched becomes Lines, and Cross-hatched becomes Crossed lines.

6 Looks overlap with Stroke style, and Custom matches what people expect Watch

Three people noticed a look name that repeats another row's value (Solid, Dashed). P5 then predicted the design's own answer.

Dashed is weird, because there's also a stroke style row that does dashed. So if I pick the Dashed look and then change stroke style, what's the look now?

P5, Q5, 07:50

It should say Custom or something. Otherwise it's lying to me.

P5, Q5, 08:47

Round 2: Filled removes the Solid overlap. Dashed stays, because the row already says Custom once a look no longer matches.

Method and limits

  • Five voice interviews with an AI moderator, 16 to 22 minutes each, about still screenshots of the real lab build. None were excluded for quality.
  • Everyone saw today's panel first, then the picked panel, then its top row open. Without the other order, recency and familiarity can't be separated from the panels.
  • All five had used Excalidraw, so familiarity with today's panel is overrepresented.
  • The guide had no unaided "make the border thicker" task, showed the look names with their pictures, and skipped the arrow looks and the Custom value.
  • Screenshots show what people expect, not what they did. The panel isn't shipped, so there's no usage data.
